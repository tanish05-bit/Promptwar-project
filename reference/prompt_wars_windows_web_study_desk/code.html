§ 03.4 Is Inductive Alignment Merely Statistical Habit?

Selected: "Hume's turkey expects grain..."

When reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) optimizes model outputs for adherence, it reproduces what David Hume famously characterized as empirical custom. The machine does not deduce universal deontic ethics; rather, like the inductive observer in menu_book Russell '12 · Ch.6 §2 , it infers benevolence strictly through repetition of rewards.

“The man who has fed the chicken every day throughout its life at last wrings its neck instead, showing that more refined views as to the uniformity of nature would have been useful to the bird.”

In modern frontier prompt architecture, benchmark saturation presents an analogous crisis. A prompt tested against 10,000 synthetic evaluations builds high inductive confidence graphic_eq Tape 24:15 . Yet, when deployed into adversarial open environments, zero-shot distribution shifts render statistical habits completely brittle. The model has internalized neither safety nor reason, but simply the expectation of continuous grain. description Lec 03 · p.12

The Fallacy of Historical Induction

Statistical alignment merely optimizes for known reward vectors. Without counterfactual meta-prompting and structural bounds, each successful run reinforces the illusion of safety until an out-of-distribution prompt triggers complete hallucination.
